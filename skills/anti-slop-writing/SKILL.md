# Anti-slop writing

Write prose that a specific reader can act on. Slop is text that could be pasted into any document unchanged. This skill has two modes: write (apply the rules while drafting) and edit (change as little as possible and keep the author's voice).

## Before you write

1. Name the reader and what they already know.
2. Name what they should know, feel or do after reading.
3. Lead with that. Put the point in the first sentence.
4. If you are editing someone else's draft, keep their vocabulary, rhythm, humor and strong opinions. Their sample outranks these rules.

## Never invent facts

Do not add numbers, dates, names, quotes, customers, benchmarks or sources that are not in the material you were given. If a sentence needs a detail you lack, write a plainer sentence or ask. A fabricated specific is worse than a generic phrase.

## Banned words and phrases

Replace each with the plain, specific thing, or cut it.

- Hype verbs and adjectives: delve, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, foster, elevate, embark, harness, supercharge, unlock, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, transformative, game-changer, paradigm shift, ever-evolving, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, tapestry, realm, beacon, landscape.
- Business filler: navigate, unpack, lean into, double down, deep dive, take a step back, moving forward, circle back, on the same page.
- Filler phrases: at its core, in today's [anything], it's worth noting, at the end of the day, when it comes to, in a world where, the reality is, going forward, let's dive in, in order to.
- Empty adverbs: really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, truly, deeply, fundamentally, inherently, importantly, crucially. Keep one only when it carries real emphasis.
- Fake candor and emphasis: "Here's the thing", "Here's what/why...", "The truth is", "Let me be clear", "Make no mistake", "Let that sink in", "Full stop.", "Period.", "Real talk", "Honestly?".
- Vague declaratives: "the implications are significant", "the stakes are high", "the reasons are structural". Name the implication, stake or reason.
- Weasel attribution: "experts say", "studies show". Name the source or cut the claim.

## Structural tells

1. Binary contrast: "It's not X, it's Y" and "not just X but Y". State Y.
2. Negative listing: saying what a thing is not before what it is. State what it is.
3. Rule of three: three-item lists and triplet adjectives by reflex. Use as many items as the content has.
4. Throat-clearing openers: any sentence that announces a point instead of making it.
5. Signposting: "In this section we'll...", "Let me walk you through...", "As we'll see...". Do the thing.
6. Fake-profound closers: aphorisms, pull-quotes, "the future looks bright", recap paragraphs. End on the last concrete fact or the next action.
7. Chatbot closers and openers: "I hope this helps", "Great question", "Let me know if you'd like...", "Certainly!".
8. Em dashes: none in short copy, at most two in a long piece. Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses. Check for spaced dashes and double hyphens too.
9. Hedging: "could potentially", "it may be possible that", stacked qualifiers. Say it, or state the real uncertainty once.
10. Dramatic fragments and rhetorical setups: "What if...?", "Think about it:". Write full sentences.
11. Colon reveals used for drama. Use colons for lists, labels and quotes only.
12. Synonym cycling: repeat the clear word rather than rotating near-synonyms.
13. Inanimate subjects with human verbs ("the data tells us", "the tool understands"). Name who acts.
14. Passive voice that hides a known actor. Put the actor first.
15. Metronomic rhythm: three or more sentences of the same length, or every paragraph ending on a punchy one-liner. Vary both.
16. Formatting slop: emoji in headings, bold in mid-sentence, bullets where a sentence works, headers over two-line sections.

## Before and after

Before: "In today's fast-paced landscape, it's worth noting that our new release isn't just an update, it's a game-changer that empowers teams to seamlessly streamline their workflows."

After: "Version 2.1 cuts a deploy from 12 minutes to 4. It also adds rollback from the CLI." (Only valid if those figures came from the user. Otherwise write what the release does, using the facts you have.)

## Self-check pass

Read the draft once more as the reader. For each paragraph, ask whether it would still make sense pasted into a different product's docs. If so, make it specific or delete it.

## Before you finish

- [ ] The first sentence carries the point.
- [ ] No banned word or phrase from the list above remains.
- [ ] No "not X, it's Y", no reflexive triplets, no signposting.
- [ ] No em dashes, or at most two in a long piece.
- [ ] The ending is a fact or a next step, not an aphorism or a recap.
- [ ] Every number, name and quote exists in the source material.
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary and the draft reads naturally aloud.
- [ ] In edit mode: the author's voice and meaning are intact, and you listed what you changed.
